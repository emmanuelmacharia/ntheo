Ntheo gallery rearchitecture

Replacing the homepage gallery with a browsable archive of the wedding weekend, ordered by when photos were actually taken.

Plan drafted 25 August 2026 343 media items, 1.39 GB Status: built, pending one poster-capture run

The defect

The database has never known the shape of the media it stores. media_table holds a URL, a MIME type, a byte size, a boolean, and a free-text tag. It has no width, no height, no capture time, no duration, and no poster frame.

Everything people complain about descends from that one gap. sharedImage.tsx forces every photo into a hardcoded 200px box with object-fit: cover, so a portrait shot loses its subject to the crop. The masonry toggle in ClientGallery.tsx has never worked, because packing a masonry layout requires aspect ratios the database cannot supply. Videos render as grey rectangles. The gallery shows the 20 most recent uploads and offers no route to the other 323.

So the work is ordered: metadata first, layout second, experience third. No interface work starts before the backfill lands, because every layout decision depends on dimensions being correct.

Uploads tell a completely different story: 21 files arrived on 2 August, 131 on 11 August, and 166 on 20 August, weeks after the event. Sorting by upload time does not approximate the day, it shatters it. That single comparison is why capture time became the spine of the whole design.

The 00 hour on 10 August is the reception on the 9th still going, which is why event days run 04:00 to 04:00 instead of midnight to midnight.

Two findings made the backfill much cheaper than expected. The CDN answers HTTP range requests, so we read 128 KB per file instead of whole originals. And each JPEG carries an embedded thumbnail inside its EXIF block, already within those bytes, which becomes the blur placeholder for free. The full pass costs about 22 MB rather than 1.39 GB.

Domain model

Three levels, defined in CONTEXT.md. An Event is an occasion whose media belongs together. A Chapter is a named, ordered stretch of one Event. A Media Item is one photo or one video.

Saturday 9 August Event 319 items, the day Midday Chapter 27 12:00 onward, real timestamps Early afternoon Chapter 10 Afternoon Chapter 119 the ceremony More from the day Chapter 163 of the day, timestamps rewritten Saturday 2 August Event 22 items, a different occasion

Chapters are proposed automatically from 40-minute gaps in capture time and then renamed by hand. The gallery is fully usable before anyone renames anything.

Schema

One new table. ntheo_event holds id, parentId, title, slug, startsAt, endsAt, sortOrder, coverMediaId, visible. A row with parentId null is an Event. A row with a parentId is a Chapter of that Event.

This started as two tables and became one. Creating ntheo_chapter failed against the live database:

The max number of tables allowed for this Database has been reached.

The database caps at 10 tables and is shared with an unrelated project that owns 5 mosaic_* tables. Ntheo's 5 fill the rest. Paying for a larger plan to store roughly a dozen rows was not worth it, so Chapters became a self-reference. See ADR-0005. Anything else Ntheo needs a table for will hit the same wall.

The orientation trap. JPEG header dimensions are recorded before rotation. A phone portrait commonly reports 4032x3024 with orientation 6 and actually displays as 3024x4032. Writing raw header values would hand justified rows the wrong aspect ratio for a large share of the library and rebuild the cropping bug behind a new route. Orientations 5 through 8 swap width and height at write time.

Backfill

Stage 1, probe

Range-fetch 128 KB per file, 343 files, about 22 MB total. Images give up EXIF capture time, dimensions, orientation, GPS, and the embedded thumbnail that sharp downscales into the LQIP. Videos give up duration, creation_time, and whether moov sits at the front.

Stage 2, display copies

The 5 NEF and 5 HEIC files get their embedded JPEG previews extracted and uploaded through UTApi into displayUrl. A failure sets visible = false rather than shipping a broken tile. This needs UPLOADTHING_TOKEN added to src/env.js, where it is currently missing despite being present in .env.

Stage 3, posters

An admin page loads each video, seeks to about one second, paints to a canvas, and uploads the frame. Viable because the CDN sends Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *, which leaves the canvas untainted. It also returns true videoWidth and videoHeight, which matters because parsing tkhd out of the QuickTime files returned nothing usable.

Stage 4, clustering

Event days bounded at 04:00. Chapters split on 40-minute gaps. Covers chosen from the middle of the densest Moment, ties broken on pixel count. Everything produced here is a proposal that can be renamed and reordered.

Routes and components

/gallery justified rows, cursor-paginated /gallery/[id] full page, real URL, OG tags /gallery/@modal/(.)[id] intercepted, modal on in-app tap /gallery/play StoryPlayer /admin/curate rename, reorder, hide, feature /admin/backfill one-time poster capture

JustifiedGrid packs rows against the real container width using aspect ratios from the database, so space is reserved before a byte downloads. MediaTile renders LQIP then image, or poster then video. VideoTile autoplays muted in view, stops at 8 seconds, allows two players at once, and pauses off-screen. MediaViewer handles keyboard, swipe, double-tap zoom, download, share, and preloads two items either side. StoryPlayer takes any list of Media Items and runs 4 seconds per image, 10 second video clips, and a title card between chapters, muted with an unmute control.

Deleted: Gallery.tsx, ClientGallery.tsx, sharedImage.tsx, sharedVideo.tsx. The homepage keeps one justified row linking to /gallery.

Risks

Netlify image optimisation. 343 originals at 2 to 7 MB routed through next/image is real transform volume against the quota. If it bites, generate fixed-width copies during the backfill and serve them straight from the CDN.

The CORS header is not ours. Poster generation depends on UploadThing sending Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *. If that ever tightens, ffmpeg becomes the fallback for existing videos.

The database is full. 10 of 10 tables used, half of them belonging to another project. Every future Ntheo feature needing storage has to reuse an existing table or displace a mosaic_* one. Splitting the projects into separate databases is the real fix whenever it becomes worth doing.

Curation is owner time, not build time. Clustering gets the structure roughly right on its own, but naming chapters and starring the good photographs is work only the owner can do. The gallery works chronologically before any of it happens.
